Day 2

Psychology Tests — TAT, WAT, SRT, SDT

Four written, projective tests taken back-to-back in one sitting. There are no right/wrong answers — the psychologist is reading the consistent personality pattern across all four, so honesty + consistency beats "the perfect answer" every time.

🖼️ TAT — 12 pictures 💬 WAT — 60 words / 15 sec each ⚡ SRT — 60 situations / 30 min 📝 SDT — 5 perspectives / 15 min

1 TAT — Thematic Apperception Test

Format: 11 pictures (hazy, like PPDT but varied themes) shown for 30 seconds each, then 4 minutes to write a story per picture; the 12th slide is blank — you imagine and write your own picture/story.

  • Same story structure as PPDT: past (lead-up) → present (action) → future (positive outcome)
  • Always make your hero achieve the goal through their own effort — not luck, not others rescuing them
  • Cover a variety of themes across your 12 stories: family, career/technical achievement, sports, adventure, social service, relationships — don't repeat the same plot
  • The blank slide is a chance to showcase a theme you feel strongly about (e.g., an IT/engineering achievement story)

2 WAT — Word Association Test

Format: 60 words flashed one at a time, ~15 seconds each. Write the very first sentence that comes to mind using that word — not a definition, not a phrase, a full simple sentence.

Write in first-person or general statements that reflect positive traits: courage, discipline, teamwork, problem-solving. Avoid negative, violent or joke responses even if they come to mind first.
WordWeak responseStronger response
Fail"Fail" (just repeats word)"Failure teaches me to try again with a better plan."
Computer"I like computers""I debug the computer program to fix the error."
Fight"I hate to fight""He fights against all odds to complete the mission."
Death(blank / panic)"Death of fear makes a soldier courageous."

3 SRT — Situation Reaction Test

Format: A booklet of ~60 everyday/crisis situations, ~30 minutes total (~30 sec/situation) to write how you would react — in first person, action-oriented, realistic.

  • Answer what you would practically do — not an idealistic/heroic fantasy, not something passive
  • Keep responses short, specific and complete (who does what, resulting in what)
  • Show presence of mind, responsibility-taking, and concern for others' safety in emergencies
SituationSample strong response
He was told that his mother was suffering from cancer. He... ...consulted specialist doctors immediately, arranged the best possible treatment and stayed positive for the family.
The college server crashed a day before submissions. He... ...quickly restored the last backup, informed the faculty of the delay, and helped classmates resubmit on time.
While trekking, his friend sprained his ankle far from help. He... ...gave first aid, made a support splint, and helped him walk slowly back to the base camp.

4 SDT — Self Description Test

Format: ~15 minutes to write 5 short paragraphs describing yourself from 5 perspectives:

  1. What your parents think of you
  2. What your teachers/employer think of you
  3. What your friends think of you
  4. What you think of yourself
  5. What you'd like to improve about yourself
Consistency check: The psychologist cross-matches your SDT with your WAT/SRT/TAT responses and your PIQ form. Don't invent a personality that contradicts your other answers — this is the single most under-rated test.
