Day 2

Psychology Tests — TAT, WAT, SRT, SDT

Four written exercises commonly used during SSB psychological assessment. Practise expressing your own thinking clearly under time pressure; there is no magic set of "model answers." Exact administration can vary by board.

🖼️ TAT — 12 pictures 💬 WAT — 60 words / 15 sec each ⚡ SRT — 60 situations / 30 min 📝 SDT — 5 perspectives / 15 min

1 TAT — Thematic Apperception Test

Typical practice format: 11 picture prompts, with about 30 seconds to observe and four minutes to write each story; a final blank slide may ask for your own scene. Follow the timings and instructions actually given by your board.

  • Use a simple sequence: situation → action or decision → plausible outcome; difficult outcomes can be realistic too
  • Use the scene's actual clues rather than forcing an officer or IT theme into every picture
  • A character can collaborate or seek help; realistic judgement matters more than solo heroics
  • For a blank slide, choose a scene from your own experience or observations that you can write about naturally

2 WAT — Word Association Test

Typical practice format: 60 words flashed one at a time, often about 15 seconds each. Write a short, meaningful thought related to each word; centre instructions take precedence.

Do not mechanically convert every word into a positive slogan. A difficult word can prompt a calm, realistic response. Write naturally, legibly and promptly; avoid memorising these illustrations.
WordSuperficial responseMore specific illustration
Delay"Delay is bad.""I informed the team early when the delivery was delayed."
Computer"I like computers.""I tested the program after fixing its login error."
Disagree"Never disagree.""We compared both proposals before settling the disagreement."
Loss"Loss is terrible.""The team reviewed what went wrong after the loss."

3 SRT — Situation Reaction Test

Typical practice format: around 60 everyday or difficult situations in roughly 30 minutes. Your actual booklet and instructions may differ. Write how you would respond within your real abilities.

  • Answer what you would practically do — not an idealistic/heroic fantasy, not something passive
  • Keep responses short, specific and complete (who does what, resulting in what)
  • Show judgement and concern for safety in emergencies; call trained help when necessary rather than improvising unsafe treatment
SituationSample strong response
His parent received a serious diagnosis. He... ...listened, helped arrange a specialist appointment, and discussed next steps with the family.
The college server crashed a day before submissions. He... ...informed the faculty and IT administrator, checked the available backup, and requested an extension if needed.
While trekking, his friend sprained his ankle far from help. He... ...stopped the trek, assessed whether movement was safe, and contacted the guide or emergency services for help.

4 SDT — Self Description Test

Typical practice format: about 15 minutes for five short self-reflection prompts. Write candidly rather than inventing other people's opinions:

  1. What your parents think of you
  2. What your teachers/employer think of you
  3. What your friends think of you
  4. What you think of yourself
  5. What you'd like to improve about yourself
Self-check: Compare your draft with real feedback you have received and your PIQ form. Do not claim traits you cannot support with examples.

5 TAT Story Workshop · 12 Original Prompts

Text descriptions are a writing drill, not substitutes for actual ambiguous pictures. Set four minutes per story; before writing, ask: What do I see? What could have happened? What might the people realistically do next?

01 · Late library

A person sits with books open under a desk lamp. A notice is visible near the door.

Review cue

Notice and books are visible; exam panic, a cancelled class, or a specific occupation are assumptions.

02 · Empty court

Two people stand at opposite ends of a sports court. Equipment lies beside a bench.

Review cue

What can you infer from equipment? How would the characters communicate or plan?

03 · Clinic doorway

A person holds a folder outside a clinic room; a second person stands near the reception counter.

Review cue

A folder does not prove illness. Keep any health-related actions cautious and plausible.

04 · Rain at the crossing

Three people wait by a pedestrian crossing as rain begins. One holds an umbrella.

Review cue

What action improves safety without inventing an accident?

05 · Workshop table

Two people examine a small device on a table while another watches from the side.

Review cue

Possible goal: inspect or test it. Do not assert a technical failure without a clue.

06 · Riverside path

A cyclist stops beside a path where a group of walkers is looking toward the river.

Review cue

Why have they stopped? Several explanations fit; choose one and act consistently.

07 · Community meeting

Four people sit around a table with papers, a pen and an empty chair.

Review cue

Show discussion, planning, and follow-through rather than instant agreement.

08 · Station platform

A traveller looks at a display board while holding a bag. Another person approaches.

Review cue

What information can be verified? Avoid assuming a missed train.

09 · Playing field

A player sits beside the field with teammates standing nearby. A water bottle is on the ground.

Review cue

Could be rest, disappointment or an injury; do not diagnose from posture alone.

10 · Computer lab

A student points at a screen while two peers look on; a teacher is at the back of the room.

Review cue

IT context is visible here; describe a feasible learning or troubleshooting step.

11 · Harbour walkway

Two people stand near a moored boat, one holding a clipboard. Water appears calm.

Review cue

Write about preparation or observation, not a made-up naval operation.

12 · Blank slide

No image is provided. Invent a modest, familiar scene from your own life or observations.

Review cue

Would you recognise the people and motivations if the same theme came up in an interview?

Draft one story

Self-review

Is the story anchored in visible evidence, coherent and believable? Did the character act within their ability? A realistic setback is not a failure of the exercise.

7 SRT Mini Set · 12 Situations in 6 Minutes

Thirty seconds per prompt approximates a common practice pace. Write short actions on paper first, then compare with the review cues; your response need not match the examples.

  1. A classmate asks you to add their name to a project they did not work on.
  2. Your team notices a minor bug just before a public demonstration.
  3. Your train is delayed and an exam starts in two hours.
  4. A teammate has not spoken during a group planning meeting.
  5. You receive conflicting directions from two supervisors.
  6. You see someone slip near a wet staircase and appear unable to stand.
  7. Your friend shares an unverified message about an emergency in your town.
  8. Your internet fails while submitting an assignment near the deadline.
  9. You realise you gave a wrong figure in a presentation.
  10. One member takes all the credit for a group result.
  11. A workshop device begins overheating while you are using it.
  12. You promised a friend help, but an urgent family task has come up.
Review cues and possible responses
  1. Explain credit must reflect work; offer them a real task instead.
  2. Assess impact, tell the team, fix if safe or disclose the limitation.
  3. Check alternatives and inform the exam authority if delay is unavoidable.
  4. Ask for their view without putting them on the spot.
  5. Clarify priority with the supervisors rather than silently choosing.
  6. Secure the area, ask about injury and get appropriate help; avoid moving them if serious injury is possible.
  7. Verify with an official source and do not forward the rumour.
  8. Try a backup connection, retain evidence, and inform the instructor promptly.
  9. Correct the figure immediately and explain the accurate source.
  10. Discuss contributions calmly with the person and raise it appropriately if necessary.
  11. Stop using it, isolate power if safe, warn others and alert a supervisor.
  12. Explain the conflict early and arrange another time or someone who can help.

8 SDT Guided Worksheet

Collect real feedback before writing. For each perspective, add one genuine strength with a concrete incident, one area to improve, and what you have actually done about it. If unsure what someone thinks, ask them rather than inventing it.

Drafts are saved only in this browser; shared devices may expose personal details. Do not enter sensitive information unless you are comfortable storing it locally.
Self-check before using your draft

Would a parent, teacher or friend recognise this description? Are examples specific and truthful? Are strengths and limitations consistent with your PIQ and interview, without trying to manufacture a flawless personality?
