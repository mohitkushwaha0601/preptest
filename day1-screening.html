Day 1

Screening — OIR Test & PPDT

Day 1 is a screening stage, not a full assessment. Practise reasoning under time pressure, observe the picture carefully, and contribute constructively to the discussion. Only screened-in candidates proceed to subsequent tests.

🧩 OIR Test (~30 min) 🖼️ Picture Perception (30 sec) ✍️ Story Writing (4 min) 🗣️ Group Discussion (~15–20 min)

1 OIR Test (Officer Intelligence Rating)

Reasoning exercises are timed; booklet structure, timing and marking instructions can vary by board. Follow the instructions given at your centre.

SectionType of QuestionsTypical Count / Time
VerbalAnalogies, classification (odd one out), coding-decoding, series, direction sense, blood relations, number/letter series, sentence completion~40–50 Qs / ~15–18 min
Non-VerbalPattern completion, mirror/water images, figure series, embedded figures, odd figure out~40 Qs / ~15 min
Strategy: Attempt easy/known-type questions first in one pass, mark unsure ones, come back if time remains. Work for both speed and accuracy; timed practice builds pattern recognition. Check your centre's instructions before making assumptions about marking.

2 PPDT — Picture Perception & Discussion Test

Step 1 — Picture Perception (30 seconds)

A briefly displayed ambiguous picture is used. Record only what you actually observe; do not force a fixed number of figures or a particular mood. Note down:

  • Number of characters, approximate age & gender
  • Mood you perceive (positive, neutral or negative)
  • One-word theme that strikes you first

Step 2 — Story Writing (4 minutes)

Write a story covering: what led to the situation, what is currently happening, and what the outcome will be. Keep it:

  • Believable actions and outcomes consistent with the image; difficult or uncertain situations are valid too
  • Age-appropriate to your own age group as the lead character
  • One clear central character with a goal, obstacle and resolution

Step 3 — Narration & Group Discussion

Individual Narration (~1 min each)

Every candidate narrates their perception & story to the group and the assessors, one by one, by chest number.

Group Discussion (~15–20 min)

The group (usually 15–20 candidates) discusses the picture and arrives at one common, agreed story. This is the real test — of your communication, assertiveness and cooperation.

What assessors are really watching in the GD: Do you initiate or wait? Do you listen and build on others' points? Can you stay assertive without shouting over people? Do you help the group reach consensus within time? Do you speak in clear, grammatically sound English/Hindi with reasonable volume?

✅ Do

Speak when you have something useful to add. Back your points with the picture's cues. Involve quieter members if you're a natural leader in the discussion. Conclude by helping frame the final common story if the group is stuck.

🚫 Don't

Don't stay silent to "play safe" or dominate by talking over everyone. Don't impose an upbeat story if the picture suggests a difficult situation; show a grounded response instead.

3 Sample Story Skeleton

Perception: "A young man (age 24) standing near a factory gate, looking thoughtfully towards the building. Mood: neutral-to-positive, contemplative."

Story: Ravi (24) has just completed his engineering degree and applied for a technical officer post. He visits the plant to understand ground realities before his interview. He notices an inefficiency in the production line, discusses it with the shift supervisor, and offers a simple fix using his coding background. The supervisor is impressed and recommends him. Ravi leaves confident, motivated to keep learning practical systems alongside theory.

Illustration only, not an answer to memorise. In the actual test your story must follow the picture's visible cues; don't force an IT theme onto unrelated scenes.

4 OIR Worked Drills

Original practice items (not past SSB questions). Try each group without opening the explanations; use the timer to rehearse pace.

Set A · Series & coding

  1. 3, 7, 15, 31, ?
  2. B, E, I, N, ?
  3. If COLD → DPME (each letter moves +1), how is SHIP written?
  4. 6, 12, 9, 18, 15, ?
Check answers and reasoning

1. 63: double and add 1. 2. T: letter positions increase by 3, 4, 5, 6. 3. TIJQ: shift each letter +1. 4. 30: alternately double and subtract 3.

Set B · Verbal logic

  1. Compass : direction :: thermometer : ?
  2. Odd one out: router, switch, firewall, keyboard.
  3. A is B's sister; B is C's father. What is A to C?
  4. Walk 3 km north, then 4 km east. How far and in what direction are you from the start?
Check answers and reasoning

1. Temperature: instrument and measurement. 2. Keyboard: the others primarily serve network traffic/security roles. 3. Aunt: father's sister. 4. 5 km northeast: use the 3–4–5 right triangle.

Set C · Pattern thinking

  1. Shape sequence: △, □, ⬟, ⬡, ? (3, 4, 5, 6 sides)
  2. Arrow turns 90° clockwise: ↑, →, ↓, ?
  3. Vertical mirror of ↗ is which arrow?
  4. Matrix rule: row 1 has 1 dot then 2 dots; row 2 has 2 dots then ? (each move right/down adds one).
Check answers and reasoning

1. Heptagon (7 sides). 2. ←. 3. ↖ (left/right reverse). 4. 3 dots. Text symbols simplify visual reasoning; practise actual figures separately.

After checking, write down which rule you missed: step size, rotation, reflection, relationship, or direction. Redo missed items tomorrow without looking.

5 PPDT Perception & Narration Lab

These are scene descriptions, not authentic blurred PPDT slides. Use them to practise writing and narration; for visual perception also practise with images you are permitted to use.

🧑‍💻 📋

Scene 1 · Campus noticeboard

A young person stands beside a noticeboard with two others nearby. A laptop is open on a table. One figure's expression is unclear.

Observe: Who is present? What evidence suggests an activity? What is unknown?

Possible story direction

A student notices a timetable change, confirms it with staff and alerts classmates so they arrive at the correct venue. Equally valid: another plausible, picture-grounded plan.

🌧️ 🚌 👥

Scene 2 · Rainy bus stop

Two people stand near a bus stopped beside a wet road. One person appears to be looking toward the bus door.

Observe: Is the bus broken? You cannot know; avoid asserting it without evidence.

Possible story direction

A commuter sees passengers struggling to board safely in heavy rain, helps organise a queue and alerts the driver to a slippery step. Keep details proportional to what is visible.

🚤 🌊 👤

Scene 3 · Jetty

A person stands near a small boat at a jetty. Another figure is partly obscured by the boat; the sky looks overcast.

Observe: What action could be sensible without assuming a storm or emergency?

Possible story direction

Before a planned crossing, a volunteer checks weather information and safety equipment with the boat operator, then postpones departure if conditions are unsafe.

Timed writing + speaking cycle

Pick one scene. Look for 30 seconds, then write for four minutes. Hide your draft and narrate aloud in roughly one minute. Ask a friend which facts they heard and which they thought you invented.

Self-review questions

Did I separate observations from assumptions? Is there a clear sequence and believable action? Could I narrate it without reading? Did I listen to others and help combine compatible ideas?
