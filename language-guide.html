Across the SSB · A working vocabulary

Words that make actions clear

An adjective bank for PPDT, TAT, WAT, SRT, self-description, interviews, GDs and lecturettes. Pick a word only when the action or experience actually supports it. Concrete verbs and outcomes do more work than impressive labels.

15 OLQs, four factors Context-specific examples Natural, not scripted

1 Start with evidence, not adjectives

My take: building a vocabulary is useful for expressing what you actually mean under time pressure. It is not a shortcut to displaying OLQs: assessors can compare what you say, write and do. Never add a trait simply because it sounds desirable.

Easy pattern: situation → action (what you did) → reason or result → a fitting descriptor, if needed.

Compare: “I am a responsible leader” is a claim. “When our project lead was absent, I divided the remaining work, checked progress with the team and submitted on time” gives the listener evidence of responsibility and organisation.

For stories: show the action; you rarely need to call the character “brave” or “intelligent.” For the interview/SDT: use a modest adjective followed by a real example and a limit or lesson. For GD/lecturettes: describe ideas and trade-offs, not yourself.

2 Factor I — Planning & organising

Use these when someone understands a problem, makes a workable plan and communicates it clearly. The words below are choices, not synonyms to stack in one sentence.

Effective intelligence

practical · resourceful · observant · adaptable · inventive · solution-focused

Show it: notices the available tools and finds a safe workaround within the constraints.

Reasoning ability

analytical · logical · discerning · methodical · evidence-led · clear-headed

Show it: checks the cause of a server failure before changing settings.

Organising ability

organised · systematic · prepared · coordinated · efficient · structured

Show it: assigns roles, fixes a timeline and follows up on pending tasks.

Power of expression

clear · concise · articulate · precise · coherent · tactful

Show it: explains the plan in two steps and checks that others understand.

3 Factor II — Social adjustment

These fit moments involving other people: listening, adapting, sharing work and owning commitments.

Social adaptability

adaptable · open-minded · receptive · respectful · approachable · considerate

Show it: changes approach after hearing a teammate’s different perspective.

Cooperation

collaborative · supportive · inclusive · helpful · cooperative · fair-minded

Show it: invites a quieter member to speak and builds on their useful idea.

Sense of responsibility

dependable · accountable · reliable · conscientious · trustworthy · safety-conscious

Show it: reports a mistake promptly and takes steps to correct it.

4 Factor III — Social effectiveness

Describe constructive initiative and influence, not volume, dominance or a flawless personality.

Initiative

proactive · enterprising · self-starting · alert · action-oriented · willing

Show it: starts a task that needs doing without waiting to be told.

Self-confidence

assured · composed · self-aware · steady · confident · poised

Show it: answers a difficult question honestly, including what they do not know.

Speed of decision

decisive · prompt · timely · prioritising · quick-thinking · judicious

Show it: chooses a safe first step with the information available, then reassesses.

Ability to influence the group

persuasive · constructive · diplomatic · encouraging · credible · consensus-building

Show it: explains why a plan works and gets the team’s input before proceeding.

Liveliness

engaged · enthusiastic · upbeat · energetic · warm · spirited

Show it: stays attentive and helps maintain morale without distracting the group.

5 Factor IV — Dynamic & achievement

Link effort to realistic constraints; courage is not recklessness, and persistence is not refusing to change a bad plan.

Determination

persistent · resilient · committed · tenacious · focused · disciplined

Show it: tries a revised method after the first attempt fails.

Courage

brave · principled · candid · resolute · calm under pressure · risk-aware

Show it: raises a safety concern despite disagreement and proposes a safer way forward.

Stamina

enduring · steady · hardworking · energetic · consistent · persevering

Show it: maintains useful effort throughout a long task and manages energy sensibly.

6 Match the word to the setting

SettingNatural wordingWhy it works
PPDT / TAT story“She checked the route, called for help and guided the group to a safe meeting point.”Actions imply observant, decisive and responsible; no adjective is required.
WAT“Careful planning helps teams use limited time well.”One clear thought; not a canned claim about your own greatness.
SRT“I moved the group away from the hazard, alerted the appropriate help and followed their instructions.”Practical sequence and safety awareness, not an implausible rescue fantasy.
SDT“My friends find me dependable because I follow through on plans; I am working on speaking up sooner in new groups.”One believable strength plus a specific area for growth.
Personal interview“I was methodical in debugging the issue: I reproduced it, narrowed the cause and tested the fix.”Claim backed by a verifiable example; adapt it to your own experience.
GD / GPE“A practical first step is to prioritise safety; we can then divide tasks based on the resources we have.”Constructive, collaborative language without dominating.
Lecturette“A balanced cyber policy should be secure, usable and accountable.”Adjectives describe the policy; follow with a reason and an example.
Precision beats intensity: “confident” is often better than “fearless”; “decisive” is not “impulsive”; “persistent” is not “stubborn”; “cooperative” is not “submissive.” Avoid sweeping claims such as “always”, “never” and “perfect”.

Quick swaps: vague → meaningful

  • “He was good” → “He was reliable: he completed his share and informed the team of delays.”
  • “She was smart” → “She was observant: she noticed the missing detail before submitting the plan.”
  • “Our idea is best” → “Our approach is practical because it uses the people and time available.”
  • “I am born to lead” → “I coordinated the work and invited feedback when the plan changed.”

7 Practice without sounding rehearsed

  1. Pick five words you would genuinely use in everyday speech. For each, recall one true incident and note the action that supports it.
  2. Rewrite one PPDT/TAT story using concrete verbs first; remove adjectives that merely praise the hero.
  3. Record a 60-second interview response about a real setback. Mention what you did differently next time.
  4. Take a GD or lecturette topic and use two precise descriptors for the ideas, each followed by a reason.
  5. Read it aloud. If it sounds memorised or unlike you, simplify it.

This is a vocabulary aid, not an official scoring key or a script for psychological tests. Accuracy, authenticity and conduct matter more than word choice.
